Over a year ago, I noticed that the ACM [Policy Against Harassment](https://www.acm.org/special-interest-groups/volunteer-resources/officers-manual/policy-against-discrimination-and-harassment) and their [Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) statement](https://www.acm.org/diversity-inclusion/about) prohibited harassment and discrimination based on race, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexual orientation, citizenship, nationality, disability, and age, but it had at least one glaring omission: **Caste**.

Caste is a millennia-old oppressive system of social stratification whose practices are predominant in South Asia and diasporic Southasian communities. It is a hierarchical system of societal segmentation whose membership is determined by birth, wherein Brahmins are placed in the privileged position as upper-caste and those assigned to its bottom rung—Dalits and Adivasis (indigenous people)—are regularly subjected to severe marginalization and persecution. Caste oppression is not just actively practiced today in countries like India ([The Wire](https://thewire.in/caste/ugly-reality-caste-violence-discrimination-urban-india), [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/india-dalits-still-feel-bottom-caste-ladder-n1239846), [The Hindu](https://frontline.thehindu.com/social-issues/social-justice/caste-denial-indian-college-campuses/article70610794.ece), [DW](https://www.dw.com/en/in-india-caste-still-defines-who-cleans-cities/a-73368510))—_e.g._, impacting its [science](https://www.nature.com/immersive/d41586-023-00015-2) and [technology](https://restofworld.org/2022/tech-india-caste-divides/) sectors—but is also prevalent within Southasian diasporic communities in [USA](https://clerk.seattle.gov/~cfpics/cf_322573f.pdf), [Canada](https://theconversation.com/how-caste-discrimination-impacts-communities-in-canada-224603), and [UK](https://web.archive.org/web/20260305233413/https://www.dsnuk.org/caste-in-the-uk/). There has been some recent successes in recognizing caste discrimination in [Seattle](https://www.npr.org/2023/02/22/1158687243/seattle-becomes-the-first-u-s-city-to-ban-caste-discrimination) and [Toronto](https://www.aljazeera.com/opinions/2023/3/10/can-toronto-help-canada-end-casteism-in-the-classroom), but dismantling caste system in the diaspora continues to face political challenges—_e.g._, in [California](https://slate.com/news-and-politics/2023/09/california-anti-caste-discrimination-bill-hindu-nationalism-hindutva.html). Caste discrimination is also prevelant within Silicon Valley and the broader tech industry ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-cisco-lawsuit/california-accuses-cisco-of-job-discrimination-based-on-indian-employees-caste-idUSKBN2423YE/), [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/07/14/opinion/caste-cisco-indian-americans-discrimination.html), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2020/10/27/indian-caste-bias-silicon-valley/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2021-03-11/how-big-tech-is-importing-india-s-caste-legacy-to-silicon-valley), [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/trapped-in-silicon-valleys-hidden-caste-system/), [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/asian-america/big-techs-big-problem-also-best-kept-secret-caste-discrimination-rcna33692), [Slate](https://slate.com/technology/2022/07/caste-silicon-valley-thenmozhi-soundararajan.html), [New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/news/q-and-a/googles-caste-bias-problem)) and unsurprisingly caste bias is also [reflected in the technology](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/10/01/1124621/openai-india-caste-bias/) this sector produces. It therefore becomes particularly important that [ACM](https://www.acm.org/) as "the world's largest educational and scientific computing society" recognizes and prohibits caste discrimination.

And we have some good news! Having noticed the omission of caste in ACM's anti-harassment policy and DEI statement, I reached out to Vanessa Murdock, the then Chair of the ACM SIGIR Executive Committee and also a close friend. In an act of allyship, that I am really grateful for, Vanessa raised this issue with the relevant folks in the ACM organization and (just in time for this year's [Dalit History Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalit_History_Month)) both ACM's anti-harassment policy and DEI statement have been updated to explicitly include caste as a protected category.

In spite of this positive news, a lot of work yet remains to ensure that our research and professional communities remain caste-free spaces which is only possible if we put our anti-casteism into active practice. And those who may be less familiar with the history of caste oppression, I invite you to learn more on this topic and become anti-caste allies.
